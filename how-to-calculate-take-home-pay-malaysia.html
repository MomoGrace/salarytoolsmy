How to Calculate Take-Home Pay in Malaysia After EPF, SOCSO, EIS and PCB

Take-home pay in Malaysia is the amount an employee receives after monthly payroll deductions. It is usually lower than gross salary because EPF, SOCSO, EIS and PCB may be deducted before salary is banked in.

Take-home pay meaning

Take-home pay is also called net salary or bank-in salary. It is the practical amount used for daily living, loan planning and savings. A job offer may show RM5,000 gross salary, but the actual salary received can be lower after deductions.

Take-home pay calculation steps

  1. Enter your gross monthly salary.
  2. Estimate employee EPF based on the applicable KWSP contribution schedule.
  3. Estimate SOCSO and EIS based on PERKESO rules and wage ceiling.
  4. Estimate PCB if income tax deduction applies.
  5. Subtract deductions from gross salary.

Why PCB changes take-home pay

PCB is not the same for everyone. It depends on payroll data, tax category, residence status, reliefs and other taxable income. This is why a simple salary percentage is not enough for precise tax estimation.

How to use this for financial planning

Use take-home pay when checking monthly affordability. For example, rent, car loan, home loan, family commitments and savings should be planned using net salary, not the salary printed on the offer letter.

FAQ

What is take-home pay in Malaysia?

Take-home pay is the amount received after employee deductions such as EPF, SOCSO, EIS and possible PCB.

Why is take-home pay lower than gross salary?

Because payroll deducts statutory contributions, taxes and sometimes other authorised deductions.

Does PCB always apply?

Not always. PCB depends on tax calculation and employee profile.

Is employer EPF deducted from my salary?

No. Employer EPF is the employer share and is not normally deducted from employee gross salary.
