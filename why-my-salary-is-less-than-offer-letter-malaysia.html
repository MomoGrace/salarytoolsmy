Why Is My Salary Less Than My Offer Letter in Malaysia?

Your salary may be less than the amount stated in your offer letter because the offer letter usually shows gross salary. The amount banked into your account is normally after deductions such as EPF, SOCSO, EIS and PCB.

The main reason: gross salary vs net salary

Most offer letters state gross monthly salary. Payroll then deducts employee contributions and possible tax before salary is paid. The final bank-in amount is therefore lower than the headline offer.

Common deductions that reduce salary

DeductionWhy it appears
EPFEmployee retirement contribution
SOCSOSocial security contribution
EISEmployment insurance contribution
PCBMonthly income tax deduction
Other deductionsUnpaid leave, advances, benefits or company deductions

Example

If your offer letter says RM4,000, you should not expect the full RM4,000 to be banked in if statutory deductions apply. The actual amount depends on payroll calculation and employee details.

What to check on your payslip

Check gross salary, EPF employee share, SOCSO, EIS, PCB, unpaid leave and any other deduction line. If something is unclear, ask HR or payroll to explain the specific payslip item.

How to avoid confusion before accepting a job

Before accepting a job, estimate take-home pay from the gross offer. This helps you compare offers fairly and avoid overcommitting to monthly expenses.

FAQ

Why is my salary lower than my offer letter?

Because offer letters usually show gross salary, while bank-in salary is after deductions.

Can my employer deduct EPF and SOCSO?

For eligible employees, payroll deducts employee statutory contributions according to applicable rules.

Should I ask HR about my payslip?

Yes. HR or payroll should explain deduction lines that are unclear.

Is lower bank-in salary always a problem?

Not necessarily. It can be normal if deductions are correct, but you should check your payslip to confirm.
