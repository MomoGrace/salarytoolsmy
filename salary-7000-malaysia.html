RM7,000 Salary After EPF, SOCSO, EIS & PCB in Malaysia

A RM7,000 monthly salary in Malaysia is not the same as your final take-home pay. Employees usually receive a lower net amount after EPF, SOCSO, EIS and possible PCB deductions. The exact amount depends on contribution schedules, tax profile, personal reliefs and payroll settings.

Quick answer for RM7,000 salary in Malaysia

If your gross salary is RM7,000 per month, your estimated take-home pay before PCB may be around RM6,188.00 using a simplified deduction estimate. This is not a final payroll figure because official EPF and SOCSO amounts may follow wage schedules and rounding rules.

This page is useful for professionals evaluating a higher offer, promotion or relocation package in Malaysia.

What deductions apply to RM7,000 salary?

EPF employee contribution

EPF is a retirement savings contribution. For many Malaysian employees below age 60, the employee share is commonly shown as 11%, while employer contribution is paid separately by the employer. However, KWSP also uses official contribution schedules and rounding rules, so payroll should not rely only on a rough percentage.

SOCSO contribution

SOCSO protects eligible employees under social security coverage. The amount is based on official PERKESO contribution schedules and wage ceiling rules. For online planning, a simplified estimate can help, but the employer payroll amount is the practical reference.

EIS contribution

EIS is related to employment insurance coverage. It is usually a smaller monthly deduction compared with EPF, but it still reduces final take-home pay. The employee and employer each have their own contribution share under PERKESO rules.

PCB / MTD

PCB, also called MTD, is the monthly tax deduction handled through payroll. RM7,000 salary usually makes PCB a more important part of the net salary estimate, but it still should not be guessed without payroll assumptions. This is why two employees with the same gross salary can receive different net amounts.

Is RM7,000 salary enough for monthly planning?

At this level, the difference between gross and net salary can affect mortgage planning, insurance, childcare and monthly investments.

For personal budgeting, it is better to start from estimated net salary, not gross salary. A salary offer can look attractive on paper, but the money available for rent, loans, groceries, family support and savings is the amount after monthly deductions.

FAQ

What is the take-home pay for a RM7,000 salary in Malaysia?

The take-home pay for a RM7,000 salary is the gross salary minus employee deductions such as EPF, SOCSO, EIS and possible PCB. A simple estimate before PCB is about RM6,188.00, but the final bank-in amount may be different because official tables, rounding and tax profile can change the result.

How much EPF is deducted from a RM7,000 salary?

A simple educational estimate uses 11% employee EPF for many Malaysian employees below age 60, which is about RM770.00 for RM7,000. Official payroll should refer to the current KWSP contribution schedule instead of relying only on a flat percentage.

Does SOCSO apply to a RM7,000 salary?

SOCSO normally applies to eligible employees under PERKESO rules. The final contribution should follow the official SOCSO contribution schedule and wage ceiling, not a rough online estimate.

Is RM7,000 salary taxable in Malaysia?

RM7,000 salary usually makes PCB a more important part of the net salary estimate, but it still should not be guessed without payroll assumptions.

Why is my bank-in salary lower than my offer letter salary?

Your offer letter usually shows gross salary. Your bank-in salary is lower because payroll may deduct EPF, SOCSO, EIS, PCB, unpaid leave, benefits or other authorised deductions.
